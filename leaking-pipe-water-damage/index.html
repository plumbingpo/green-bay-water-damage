Leaking Pipe Water Damage in Green Bay: What to Know

A leaking pipe rarely announces itself. Water seeps out behind drywall, at the back of a sink cabinet or under the concrete until a stain, a soft floor or an unexplained water bill gives it away. Often that means weeks of moisture have already gone into whatever surrounds the pipe. For Green Bay homes, our crew maps where the water traveled and dries the hidden damage along with the visible damage.

Fixing the pipe ends the leak, yet the moisture already inside the wall or floor stays put. That is our part of the job. With meters and a thermal camera we locate every wet material, make only the openings required, then keep drying equipment running until moisture levels read normal again. A slow leak gives mold plenty of time to begin, so we inspect for growth too and treat any surface it has reached.

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut Off the WaterWhen a toilet or sink line is dripping, shut the valve on the supply right at that fixture. Not sure where it is coming from? Close the main shutoff for the whole house.
  2. Stay Clear of ElectricityIf water is near outlets, switches or a light fixture, switch off the breaker for that area. Leave any wet cord or electrical device alone.
  3. Read the MeterWith every tap off, look at the meter. Movement on the dial means water is escaping out of sight, perhaps beneath the slab.
  4. Empty and PhotographClear out the cabinet or closet near the leak and take photos of stains, warped materials and the pipe itself before anything is repaired or thrown away.
  5. Have the Area MeteredAsk for meter readings of the area before anyone patches it closed. Inside the cavity, moisture can remain even when the wall surface feels dry.
Our Process

How We Handle Leaking Pipe Water Damage

1

Find Every Wet Area

Every wet area gets marked after a scan of the walls, cabinets and floors with meters and thermal imaging, and that includes places water followed the framing to, some distance from the leak.

2

Make Targeted Openings

Small, planned openings give us access to wet cavities. Insulation, drywall or cabinet pieces beyond saving come out, and moldy material is bagged to keep spores from spreading through the house.

3

Structural Drying

We keep dehumidifiers and air movers going until the subfloor and framing read normal, which usually takes 3 to 5 days. On every visit we take new readings and reposition equipment as things dry out.

4

Treat and Document

Once the surfaces involved are treated with an antimicrobial, we hand over our photos and moisture logs. That paperwork proves the area was dry ahead of repairs and can support a claim.

Coverage

Does Insurance Cover It?

A slow leak and a sudden one are not handled the same way by insurance. Coverage is common for accidental, sudden damage, while a leak that ran for months or weeks is usually not covered and is specifically excluded in some policies. A hidden leak you had no way of knowing about may be handled more favorably than one that was visible and ignored. Read your policy and talk to your agent. So the adjuster can work from solid facts, we provide photos, moisture readings and notes on the leak source.

Cost

What Affects the Cost

  • The location of the leak, because a wall or slab costs more to reach than an open cabinet.
  • The type of flooring and finishes involved, since some dry in place and others do not.
  • How many rooms or levels the water reached, including a ceiling below the leak.
  • What has to be torn out and put back, from drywall and insulation to cabinets and flooring.

Common Causes

What Causes It

  • Pipes in exterior walls or crawl spaces that crack after a freeze and seep once they thaw.
  • Older copper pipe that corrodes from the inside until a pinhole opens in the wall of the line.
  • Shifting soil or abrasion wearing through water lines that run beneath a concrete slab.
  • Loose or worn compression fittings and supply connections under sinks and behind toilets.
  • High water pressure that stresses joints and valves year after year until one weeps.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Rotted Framing

After weeks of dampness, bottom plates and wall studs start to go soft and rot. Any piece too weak to carry load gets cut out and swapped for new lumber.

Ceiling and Drywall Damage

A pipe leaking above a ceiling or inside a wall leaves brown rings and bubbled paint. Drywall that stays wet goes soft, sags and may need to be cut out.

Damaged Flooring

A leak in a wall or under the slab sends water beneath the floor covering. The result is cupped hardwood, laminate with swollen seams, and vinyl or tile coming loose as its adhesive fails.

Wet Insulation

Batt insulation absorbs a leak and keeps that moisture pressed to the studs. It dries very slowly in a closed cavity, so saturated insulation is usually pulled out.
